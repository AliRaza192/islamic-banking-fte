**Version:** 1.0.0 | **Sources:** AAOIFI, Quran, Sunnah, scholarly consensus

## THE 3 MAJOR PROHIBITIONS (Haram Elements to Avoid)

### 1. RIBA (ربا) — Interest / Usury
**Definition:** Any predetermined, guaranteed return on a loan or debt, regardless
of the outcome of the underlying activity.

**Quranic Reference:**
"Allah has permitted trade and forbidden Riba." — Al-Baqarah 2:275

**Two Types:**
- **Riba al-Nasi'ah:** Interest on loans (most common form — all bank interest)
- **Riba al-Fadl:** Unequal exchange of same commodity (e.g., 1kg gold for 1.1kg gold)

**What Constitutes Riba in Banking:**
- Fixed interest on deposits ❌
- Interest on loans ❌
- Penalty interest on late payment ❌
- Bank overdraft interest ❌
- Credit card interest ❌
- Compound interest ❌

**Islamic Alternative:**
- Profit from genuine trade (Murabaha) ✅
- Rent from asset ownership (Ijara) ✅
- Profit-sharing from partnership (Musharakah, Mudarabah) ✅

**Key Rule:** The return must be tied to actual asset ownership, genuine risk,
or real economic activity — not merely the passage of time on a debt.

---

### 2. GHARAR (غرر) — Excessive Uncertainty / Ambiguity

**Definition:** Unacceptable uncertainty or ambiguity in a contract that could
lead to dispute or one party being harmed.

**Examples of Gharar:**
- Selling fish not yet caught ❌
- Selling unborn animals ❌
- Insurance contract where outcome is unknown ❌ (conventional)
- Futures and options contracts (most forms) ❌
- Contract with unknown price ❌
- Contract with unknown delivery date ❌

**Permissible Uncertainty:**
- Normal business uncertainty (market prices may change) ✅
- Salam (advance payment for future delivery — allowed by exception) ✅
- Takaful (pooled mutual risk sharing — allowed as it's not bilateral) ✅

**Rule of Thumb:** Both parties must clearly know what they are buying/selling,
at what price, when it will be delivered, and in what condition.

---

### 3. MAYSIR (ميسر) — Gambling / Speculation

**Definition:** Transactions where one party's gain is directly at the expense
of another's loss, based purely on chance with no productive economic activity.

**Examples of Maysir:**
- Casino gambling ❌
- Lottery ❌
- Pure speculation in financial instruments ❌
- Conventional derivatives (used purely for speculation) ❌
- Short selling ❌

**Distinction from Permitted Risk:**
- Normal business risk (launching a product) ✅
- Investment in halal business (may lose money) ✅
- Musharakah (loss is real business loss, not zero-sum) ✅

## KEY SHARIAH PRINCIPLES (Positive Requirements)

### 1. Al-Ghunm bil Ghurm (الغنم بالغرم)
**"Profit comes with risk"**
You cannot earn a return without bearing genuine risk. The bank must truly
own the asset or invest capital before earning profit.

**Application:** Bank must purchase the car BEFORE selling it in Murabaha.
Bank must own the house BEFORE leasing it in Ijara.

---

### 2. Al-Kharaj bil Daman (الخراج بالضمان)
**"Return comes with liability"**
Whoever bears the liability (ownership risk) is entitled to the return.

**Application:** If a leased car is damaged through no fault of customer,
the bank (as owner) bears the loss — customer only pays rent for usage.

---

### 3. La Darar wa la Dirar (لا ضرر ولا ضرار)
**"No harm shall be inflicted or reciprocated"**
Financial products must not cause unfair harm to any party.

**Application:** Penalizing poor customers with compounding interest on missed
payments causes unjust harm — Islamic banks use charitable donations instead.

## SPECIFIC RULES BY PRODUCT TYPE

### Murabaha Rules
✅ Bank must genuinely own the asset before selling
✅ Actual cost price must be disclosed to customer
✅ Profit margin agreed upfront and cannot change
✅ Asset must be halal (cannot finance alcohol, pork, weapons, etc.)
❌ Cannot be used for cash loans (no "paper Murabaha" or tawarruq if circular)
❌ Cannot charge penalty interest for late payment
❌ Price cannot be variable after contract signing

### Ijara (Lease) Rules
✅ Bank must own and maintain the asset throughout
✅ Rental payments are for usufruct (use), not the asset itself
✅ Bank bears major maintenance costs (not normal wear)
✅ Bank must have Takaful on the asset
❌ Cannot lease non-existent asset (except Ijara Mawsoofa — under specific rules)
❌ Cannot lease for haram purposes
❌ Cannot make rental conditional on customer's creditworthiness only

### Musharakah Rules
✅ All partners contribute capital (or equivalent)
✅ Profit ratio agreed upfront as percentage (not fixed amount)
✅ Losses must be shared in proportion to capital
❌ Cannot guarantee profit to any partner
❌ One partner cannot bear ALL the loss (unless negligence)
❌ Cannot pre-fix a guaranteed buyout price

### Mudarabah Rules
✅ Capital provider (Rab-ul-Maal) provides only capital
✅ Manager (Mudarib) provides only expertise/labor
✅ Profit shared as per agreed ratio
✅ Loss borne by capital provider (unless Mudarib's negligence)
❌ Mudarib cannot be guaranteed any fixed return
❌ Capital provider cannot interfere in management
❌ Mudarib cannot commingle with own funds (without permission)

## HALAL SCREENING — WHAT CAN ISLAMIC FINANCE NOT FUND?

### Absolutely Haram Sectors (Cannot Finance Under Any Circumstances)
- Alcohol production, distribution, or retail ❌
- Pork or pork products ❌
- Conventional banking and insurance (interest-based) ❌
- Weapons of mass destruction ❌
- Pornography ❌
- Gambling and casinos ❌
- Nightclubs ❌
- Drugs (illegal) ❌

### Generally Impermissible (Scholarly Consensus)
- Tobacco (majority view: discouraged to prohibited) ❌
- Interest-based financial instruments ❌
- Short selling ❌
- Conventional insurance ❌

### Permissible with Conditions
- Defense industry (permissible weapons, not WMDs) ⚠️
- Entertainment (halal only — no alcohol, music industry varies) ⚠️
- Hotels (if no alcohol served) ⚠️
- Pharmaceuticals (if no haram ingredients) ⚠️
- Media (clean content only) ⚠️

### Clearly Permissible
- Food and agriculture (halal certified) ✅
- Real estate ✅
- Manufacturing ✅
- Technology ✅
- Healthcare ✅
- Education ✅
- Retail (halal products) ✅
- Construction ✅
- Transport ✅

## LATE PAYMENT — ISLAMIC BANK POLICY

**Problem:** Conventional banks charge compound penalty interest for late payments.
This is Riba — completely forbidden.

**Islamic Solution:**
1. Customer may be asked to donate to charity (not paid to bank)
2. Bank may charge actual administrative costs only
3. Bank may take legal action for recovery
4. Bank may require security/collateral (sell if default)
5. Ta'widh (compensation) — some scholars allow actual proven loss compensation

**Rule:** Bank cannot BENEFIT from late payments. Any late payment charge must
go to charity, not bank's revenue.

## SHARIAH SUPERVISORY BOARD (SSB)

**What It Is:** Independent committee of qualified Islamic scholars that approves
and monitors all products of an Islamic bank.

**Role:**
- Approve new products (check Shariah compliance)
- Issue Fatwas for specific transactions
- Annual Shariah audit of all transactions
- Answer Shariah queries from bank staff

**In Pakistan:** All Islamic banks must have an SSB per SBP requirements.
Members must be SBP-approved Shariah scholars.

**Key Pakistani Shariah Scholars (reference, not exhaustive):**
- Mufti Taqi Usmani (most influential in Islamic finance globally)
- Dr. Muhammad Imran Ashraf Usmani
- Mufti Muhammad Hassan Kaleem
- Sheikh Nizam Yaquby (international)

## COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — SHARIAH CLARIFICATIONS

**"Islamic banking is just conventional banking with different names"**
Response: The difference is structural. Bank must own the asset, bear ownership
risk, and link return to real economic activity. The legal and economic substance
is different, even if payment amounts may look similar.

**"Murabaha profit is just interest by another name"**
Response: Conventional interest accrues on a debt. Murabaha profit is fixed at
the time of sale, disclosed, and compensates the bank for owning and selling
an actual commodity. Key difference: Riba grows over time; Murabaha profit is
fixed forever at contract signing.

**"If I miss a payment, Islamic bank charges the same as conventional"**
Response: Islamic bank cannot charge compound interest. It may ask for a
charitable donation, but that donation goes to charity — not to the bank's profit.

**"Takaful is just insurance with an Arabic name"**
Response: Conventional insurance is a commercial contract where company profits
from risk. Takaful is a mutual arrangement — participants donate to a pool that
helps fellow participants. The operator manages the pool for a fee (Wakala) but
doesn't profit from risk itself.
