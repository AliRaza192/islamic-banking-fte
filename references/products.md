**Version:** 1.0.0 | **Jurisdiction:** Pakistan (SBP) + Gulf Markets
**Last Updated:** May 2026

## 1. MURABAHA (مرابحة) — Cost-Plus Financing

### What It Is
Bank purchases an asset on customer's behalf, then sells it to the customer at
cost + disclosed profit margin. Payment can be lump sum or installments.

### Key Features
- Profit rate fixed at contract signing — cannot change
- Cost price must be fully disclosed to customer
- Bank must own the asset before selling to customer
- Most common Islamic banking product in Pakistan

### Use Cases
- Car financing (most common use)
- Consumer goods (home appliances, electronics)
- Raw material financing for businesses
- Import financing

### AAOIFI Standard
FAS 2 — Murabaha and Other Deferred Payment Sales

### SBP Guidelines
IBD Circular No. 2 of 2008 — Murabaha product guidelines
Profit rate ceiling: As per SBP Consumer Financing Regulations

### Pakistan Banks Offering
All major Islamic banks — Meezan Bank, Dubai Islamic, Bank Islami, Al Baraka,
Faysal Bank Islamic, MCB Islamic, HBL Islamic

### Shariah Requirements
- Must be genuine asset sale (not cash loan)
- Commodity must be halal
- Bank bears ownership risk (even briefly)
- No penalty on late payment (except charitable donation)

### Simple Example (PKR)
```
Car price:        Rs. 3,000,000
Bank profit (18%): Rs. 1,620,000
Total payable:    Rs. 4,620,000
Tenure:           3 years (36 installments)
Monthly payment:  Rs.   128,333
```

## 2. IJARA (إجارة) — Islamic Leasing

### What It Is
Bank purchases an asset and leases it to the customer for agreed rental payments.
Customer may or may not own the asset at the end (depends on variant).

### Variants
**Ijara (Pure Lease):** Customer rents, no ownership transfer
**Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek (IMB):** Lease ending in ownership — most common
**Ijara Mawsoofa fil Dhimma:** Forward lease (for under-construction property)

### Use Cases
- Home financing (most common for property in Pakistan)
- Vehicle leasing
- Equipment leasing for businesses
- Office space financing

### AAOIFI Standard
FAS 8 — Ijarah and Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek

### SBP Guidelines
IBD Circular — Housing Finance guidelines
SBP Prudential Regulations for Islamic Banks

### Key Difference from Murabaha
In Ijara, bank retains ownership and bears ownership risks/costs (insurance,
major repairs). In Murabaha, ownership transfers immediately at sale.

### Shariah Requirements
- Bank must own asset throughout lease period
- Rental payments tied to usufruct (benefit), not loan
- Bank responsible for major maintenance
- Asset must be specified and deliverable
- Cannot lease money or consumable items

### Simple Example — Home Financing (PKR)
```
Property value:    Rs. 10,000,000
Customer deposit:  Rs.  2,000,000 (20%)
Bank finances:     Rs.  8,000,000
Monthly rental:    Rs.    120,000 (variable, linked to benchmark)
Tenure:            20 years
Ownership transfer: At end of lease via gift (Hiba) or nominal sale
```

## 3. DIMINISHING MUSHARAKAH (مشاركة متناقصة) — Declining Partnership

### What It Is
Bank and customer jointly own an asset. Customer gradually buys bank's share
over time while paying rent on bank's remaining share. Most popular for
home financing in Pakistan.

### How It Works
1. Bank + customer buy property together (e.g., 80:20 ratio)
2. Customer pays monthly rent on bank's 80% share
3. Customer also buys small portion of bank's share each month
4. Bank's share decreases, customer's increases
5. When customer buys 100% — full ownership transferred

### AAOIFI Standard
FAS 4 — Musharakah Financing

### Use Cases
- Home financing (primary use in Pakistan)
- Business premises financing
- Large equipment co-ownership

### Shariah Requirements
- Both parties share ownership genuinely
- Rental must be market-based and fair
- Customer's purchase of bank's units must be separate from rental agreement
- No pre-agreed buyout price at contract start (price set at time of purchase)

### Simple Example (PKR)
```
Property:          Rs. 10,000,000
Bank share (80%):  Rs.  8,000,000
Customer share (20%): Rs. 2,000,000

Month 1:
  Rent on bank's 80%: Rs. 96,000
  Unit purchase:      Rs. 50,000
  Total payment:      Rs. 146,000
  Bank's new share:   79.5%

...continues until bank share = 0%
```

## 4. MUSHARAKAH (مشاركة) — Full Partnership

### What It Is
Bank and customer enter a joint venture / partnership. Both contribute capital,
both share profits AND losses as per agreed ratio.

### Variants
**Permanent Musharakah:** Long-term partnership (equity-like)
**Diminishing Musharakah:** See above (bank's share reduces over time)
**Running Musharakah:** For working capital — revolving facility

### Use Cases
- SME business financing
- Project financing
- Working capital for businesses
- Trade financing

### Profit/Loss Sharing
- Profits: Shared as per agreed ratio (e.g., 60:40)
- Losses: Shared strictly in proportion to capital contribution
- Cannot pre-guarantee profit to any party

### AAOIFI Standard
FAS 4 — Musharakah Financing

### Shariah Requirements
- Both parties contribute capital
- Both share in losses (cannot shift loss entirely to one party)
- Profit ratio agreed upfront, but NOT guaranteed
- Management rights can be assigned to one party

## 5. MUDARABAH (مضاربة) — Profit-Sharing Investment

### What It Is
One party provides capital (Rab-ul-Maal), other party provides expertise/labor
(Mudarib). Profits shared as agreed; losses borne by capital provider only
(unless Mudarib was negligent).

### Roles
**Rab-ul-Maal (Capital Provider):** Investor / bank depositor
**Mudarib (Manager):** Bank (in deposit accounts) or entrepreneur (in financing)

### Use Cases
**Deposits side:** Savings accounts, term deposits in Islamic banks
**Financing side:** Financing to entrepreneurs and businesses

### How Islamic Bank Deposits Work (Mudarabah)
- You deposit money → You are Rab-ul-Maal
- Bank invests it → Bank is Mudarib
- Profit shared (e.g., 70% you, 30% bank) based on actual earnings
- No guaranteed return (unlike conventional fixed deposits)
- If bank makes loss (rare) → you bear loss, bank loses effort

### AAOIFI Standard
FAS 3 — Mudarabah Financing

### Shariah Requirements
- Capital provider cannot interfere in day-to-day management
- Losses borne solely by capital provider (unless misconduct)
- Profit ratio must be a percentage (not fixed amount)
- Cannot guarantee return to investor

## 6. SALAM (سلم) — Forward Commodity Sale

### What It Is
Buyer pays full price now for commodity to be delivered in future. Opposite of
credit sale — advance payment, future delivery.

### Use Cases
- Agricultural financing (wheat, rice, cotton before harvest)
- Commodity trading
- Working capital for farmers

### Key Rules
- Full payment at contract signing (no partial advance)
- Commodity fully specified (quality, quantity, grade)
- Delivery date fixed
- Cannot be used for gold, silver, or currency

### AAOIFI Standard
FAS 7 — Salam and Parallel Salam

### Pakistan Context
Used by Islamic microfinance banks for agricultural financing
SBP allows Salam for crop financing

## 7. ISTISNA (استصناع) — Manufacturing / Construction Finance

### What It Is
Contract to manufacture or construct a specific asset. Bank commissions
manufacturer, then sells completed asset to customer.

### Use Cases
- Construction financing (homes under construction)
- Industrial equipment manufacturing
- Infrastructure projects
- Software/IT development (contemporary application)

### Difference from Salam
- Salam: commodity already exists in nature (agricultural)
- Istisna: asset must be manufactured/built to specification

### AAOIFI Standard
FAS 10 — Istisna and Parallel Istisna

### Pakistan Context
Used by Islamic banks for home construction financing
HBFC Islamic uses Istisna for self-construction projects

## 8. SUKUK (صكوك) — Islamic Bonds / Certificates

### What It Is
Investment certificates representing ownership in tangible assets, usufructs,
or services. The Islamic equivalent of bonds — but backed by real assets,
not debt.

### Types in Pakistan
**Ijara Sukuk:** Most common — backed by lease assets
**Musharakah Sukuk:** Backed by partnership in project
**Murabaha Sukuk:** Backed by commodity sale receivables (controversial)

### Government Sukuk (Pakistan)
GOP (Government of Pakistan) issues Ijara Sukuk regularly
Listed on Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX)
Purchased by Islamic banks to meet SLR (Statutory Liquidity Requirement)

### AAOIFI Standard
SS 17 — Investment Sukuk

### Minimum Investment
GOP Sukuk: Usually Rs. 100,000 minimum for retail
Corporate Sukuk: Varies by issuer

## 9. TAKAFUL (تكافل) — Islamic Insurance

### What It Is
Mutual risk-sharing arrangement based on donation (Tabarru). Participants
contribute to a pool; pool pays out to those who suffer loss. No conventional
insurance company profit at expense of policyholders.

### Models Used in Pakistan
**Wakala Model:** Operator manages fund for a fee (most common in Pakistan)
**Mudarabah Model:** Operator shares in investment profit
**Hybrid (Wakala-Mudarabah):** Combined model

### Products Available
- Life Takaful (Hayat Takaful)
- General Takaful (property, vehicle, health)
- Family Takaful (savings + protection)

### Operators in Pakistan
- Pak-Qatar Family Takaful
- Pak-Qatar General Takaful
- EFU Life (window Takaful)
- Jubilee Life (window Takaful)
- TPL Trakker (vehicle Takaful)

### AAOIFI Standard
FAS 12 — General Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements of Islamic
Insurance Companies

### SECP Regulation
Takaful Rules 2012 (SECP) — governs all Takaful operators in Pakistan

## 10. QARD HASAN (قرض حسن) — Benevolent Loan

### What It Is
Interest-free loan given purely for the borrower's benefit. Lender expects
ONLY the principal back — no profit, no return.

### Use Cases
- Microfinance for the poor
- Emergency loans to employees
- Loans to close family
- Islamic banks' current accounts (deposits treated as Qard Hasan)

### Important Note
Current account deposits in Islamic banks are Qard Hasan — bank guarantees
return of full amount but pays no profit (unlike Mudarabah savings accounts).

### Shariah Requirements
- Absolutely zero return to lender (even indirect benefit is questionable)
- Any benefit flowing to lender makes it Riba
- Lender can accept voluntary gift AFTER repayment (not pre-agreed)
